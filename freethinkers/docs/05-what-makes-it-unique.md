# What Makes This the Only Site Like It — And What Pays For Itself

Researched August 2026. Costs and legal points are sourced at the bottom.

---

## 1. The actors idea — one part works, one part is illegal

You described a commercial: an attractive person using and enjoying the piece.
That instinct is right, and it's the single highest-converting format for
physical goods. But there's a hard split in how you produce it.

**Real creators: legal, priced, and effective.**
UGC (user-generated content) creators film exactly this. 2026 rates:

| Tier | Per video |
|---|---|
| Entry | $50–150 |
| Intermediate (what you want) | $150–300 |
| Top | $350–3,000 |

Market average lands near **$198 per deliverable**. Usage rights add **50–150%**
on top depending on term, and 5+ video bundles get **15–25% off**. Rates are per
deliverable, *not* per follower — you're buying footage, not reach.

So a starter cast of 3 creators × 2 videos ≈ **$900–1,800 + usage rights.**

**AI-generated actors: do not do this.**
The FTC's May 2026 guidance treats AI-generated testimonials as **fake reviews —
prohibited regardless of disclosure**, because a testimonial must reflect the
honest opinion of a real person who actually used the product. A synthetic
person "enjoying" your sweatshirt is a fake endorsement, full stop. Synthetic
presenters that make *no* product claim require *double* disclosure (paid, and
AI-generated). The exposure isn't worth it, and "AI art brand caught faking
customers" is the one story that would actually damage you.

**The cheaper answer you already built.** Every master ships with a QR garment.
Those buyers are real customers who can legally say anything they want. Don't
hire a cast — *make your buyers the cast*, and hire 2–3 UGC creators only to
seed the format so buyers have something to imitate.

## 2. The mechanic nobody else has: the scan economy

Your QR codes are currently decoration. Turn them into a sales force.

Each garment's QR encodes **the piece and its owner**. When a stranger scans a
sweatshirt in a coffee shop and later buys, that sale is attributed to the owner
who was wearing it. The owner earns credit — store credit, a free piece at
milestones, or cash.

Why this is genuinely unique: every art site sells art. None of them turn each
buyer into an attributed, compensated storefront that walks around a city. It
compounds — every sale adds another walking billboard with a real financial
reason to wear it.

It also solves your marketing problem permanently. You stop buying reach and
start *paying only for reach that converted.*

**Economics:** garment ≈ $32–46 landed (already budgeted). A 10% owner
commission on a $300 referral is $30 — paid only on a sale that already netted
you ~$240. Compare to $198 for one UGC video that may convert nothing.

---

## 3. "Own your day" — the gift hook

You have a 365-day calendar. Nobody else does. Let people buy **a specific
date**: a birthday, an anniversary, the day a kid was born.

- Turns an art purchase into a **gift purchase**, which is a far larger market
  and has a built-in occasion.
- Makes the calendar a **storefront**, not a progress bar.
- Gives every one of your 365 pieces a buyer with a personal reason, not just
  aesthetic preference.
- "The day you were born, rendered." sells at $300 in a way "abstract piece
  #147" does not.

This is the highest-revenue idea in this document and it's nearly free — the
calendar component already exists.

---

## 4. The provenance wall

Certificate pages already prove ownership. Make the roster public (opt-in): who
owns which day. Collecting becomes visible, and visible collecting is
competitive. This is why sneaker and watch communities work.

Pairs with the Vault: at 12 pieces you're on the wall *and* inside the vault.

---

## 5. What I'd actually skip

- **Three POD vendors.** One (Printful) until volume justifies a second.
- **NFT minting.** The audience that wants provenance is being served by your
  certificate + fingerprint. Chain adds cost, complexity, and a credibility
  problem with art buyers in 2026.
- **Sculpture section before launch.** Different shipping, insurance, and
  one-of-one pricing. The `kind` column is in the catalog so it's ready when
  you are.

---

## Sequencing, honestly

None of this matters until someone can pay you. The order that compounds:

1. **This week:** 180 pieces live, descriptions written, checkout working, one
   real sale.
2. **Week 2:** "Own your day" on the calendar. Cheapest revenue in this doc.
3. **Week 3:** scan attribution live; first garments ship with owner codes.
4. **Week 4:** 2–3 UGC creators seed the format. Buyers imitate it free.
5. **Later:** provenance wall, physical work, comics.

Steps 2 and 3 are what make it unlike anything else. Step 1 is what makes any
of it real.
